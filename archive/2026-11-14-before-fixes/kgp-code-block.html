Somewhere in the last few years

You did not lose distance. You lost the body that produced it.

The back nine got longer. Your lead hip stopped rotating the way it used to, so your lower back quietly started covering for it, and it did a decent job right up until it did not. You warm up more than you ever have and it helps less. The driver that used to be automatic now depends on the day.

Or it is sharper than that. A shoulder that complains on the follow through. An elbow that starts arguing every time you hit off a mat. Something in the low back that shows up on the drive home and is gone by Tuesday, which is exactly why you have not done anything about it.

The swing did not break. The engine did.

What you were told

Push through it. Warm up better. Play more golf.

None of that is a plan. Pain is a stop signal, and "it only hurts sometimes" is not a green light, it is an earlier warning than most people get.

Nobody raises this with you before it tears, because golf culture only talks about the body after something has already gone wrong. We start there instead.

Why it keeps happening

Golf fitness sold you a costume

Walk into most golf performance programs and you will find a resistance band, a golf posture, and a movement that looks like a swing performed slowly in a room with rubber flooring and no ball. It sells because it looks specific. It fails because specificity was never the same thing as usefulness.

This is the uncanny valley of golf fitness. Close enough to the real swing to feel purposeful. Far enough from it to be useless, and occasionally worse than useless, because you are now rehearsing a pattern under load that your body cannot yet produce cleanly. Best case, the gym version and your real swing quietly drift apart. Worst case, you have grooved a compensation under load, and it shows up exactly when you cannot afford it.

Then there is the screen. Cones, bands, a scored battery, a printout telling you which box you failed.

Screens measure how good you are at doing the screen. Practice the reach enough times and the number improves whether or not one thing changed inside your body. The test got smarter. You did not.

None of this was your fault. It was confident, it was everywhere, and it looked like science.

The argument

Your swing tells us where your body runs out of room

We watch video of your swing and we ask what you feel, and exactly where. Not to score it. To find the physical limits it is already working around.

Where the hip stops rotating. Where the rib cage stops moving and the shoulder starts stealing rotation from somewhere it should not. Where the sequence falls apart once real speed enters. That is a list of physical problems, and physical problems get solved under load in a gym.

We do not coach the swing. That is your swing coach's job, and we work behind them.

Programming stages
Competence, power, autonomy.

Both sides run

Speed on one side. Pain on the other. Most people need both.

Power and speed

Medicine ball throws, jumps, sprints, loaded carries. Explosive, athletic, and still nowhere near a swing pattern, because the goal is output your nervous system can point anywhere, not a rehearsed imitation of one movement.

  • Rotational throws at intensities that actually build power
  • Jumps and sprints for output, not for looks
  • Loaded carries and bracing under fatigue
  • General patterns: squat, hinge, push, pull, rotate

Pain and recovery

Triage runs before any loading decision gets made. Once it clears, the corrective work is specific to what your assessment actually showed, not a page of golfer's stretches copied out of a pamphlet.

  • An elbow that complains when you hit off mats: settle it first, then build load back through whatever sets it off
  • Guarding shoulder: rib cage and thoracic work to give the rotation back
  • Progress tracked against tissue response, not a calendar
  • Clinician restrictions respected, and referrals welcome

Programming stages

Three stages, not vibes

Where you start depends on what the assessment found, not on how long you have been playing.

01

Competence

Figuring out what the hell you are doing

Fundamental patterns, minimal variety, lower intensity. Quality is the only metric that counts. If your assessment flagged a limiter, it gets fixed inside this work rather than bolted on as a golf exercise. A hip that will not rotate cleanly gets hip mobility work. It does not get a weighted swing pattern designed to force rotation through a joint that is not ready for it.

02

Power

Suspiciously functional

Power and speed enter. Jumps, throws, sprints, loaded carries, built on general patterns rather than swing shapes. This is the stage built to let club head speed show up on its own, which surprises people who expected to earn it by swinging something heavy.

03

Athlete

Weaponized golfer

Fundamental patterns automatic and reliable under fatigue. Full exercise menu, real periodization, real load. This stage is built so the rotation is yours to use, the power shows up when you ask for it, and eighty swings plus four miles of walking stop costing you the next two days. You are an athlete who happens to play golf, which was the point from the first session.
